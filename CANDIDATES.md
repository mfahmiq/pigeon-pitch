# Candidates and tournament

Competition: `u-donrulezz-pigeon-pitch-one-line-af06c6cf` — one line, max
200 characters, in English: an original elevator pitch a city pigeon gives
investors for its startup.

Judge brief, criteria in weight order:
1. Delivers a one-line elevator pitch from a city pigeon to investors for its startup (off-subject loses).
2. Is the funnier of the two, with a real joke that lands rather than a mild pun or straight description.
3. Original and surprising, with an idea or twist the reader has not met before.
4. Keeps the pigeon's voice and city life in view, so the pitch could only come from a pigeon.
5. Fits one line in English within 200 characters, with every word earning its place.

A good pun counts when it lands, and a dead one does not.

## Tournament bracket

Pairwise, single elimination, judged against the brief criteria in weight
order at every match. 15 matches total.

### Round 1 (8 matches)

- **M1: C2 beats C1.** Both on-subject; C2's "poop on the competition.
  Literally." is a joke that lands, while CooWork leans on a mild pun.
- **M2: C3 beats C4.** CrumbCoin's "a crumb that never crashes" is the more
  surprising twist; the realty pitch's "tenants who never leave" is a
  familiar landlord joke.
- **M3: C5 beats C6.** "200 of us arrive in 30 seconds" is vivid and could
  only come from a pigeon flock; FlockAI's brain idea is clever but the
  joke is thinner.
- **M4: C7 beats C8.** The anti-poop subscription is a complete joke with a
  real concept; the social network's "zero trolls, one very proud statue"
  is a mild pun.
- **M5: C10 beats C9.** "Seed round — literally, seeds" is the sharper,
  investor-targeted joke; PigeonBnB's "guests who never check out" is a
  known Airbnb-joke pattern.
- **M6: C12 beats C11.** "98% of our users mate for life, and so do our
  investors" is the better twist; the mayor-eviction line is funny but
  narrower.
- **M7: C14 beats C13.** StepCoo's "investors, can you keep up?" lands as a
  closer; the weather-forecast claim reads more like a description.
- **M8: C15 beats C16.** The surveillance angle ("eyes on every street") is
  more original; the baguette pitch is a generic fundraising line.

### Quarter-finals (4 matches)

- **Q1: C2 beats C3.** The poop joke lands harder than the crypto pun; both
  are original, but C2 is funnier (criterion 2 decides).
- **Q2: C7 beats C5.** PigeonPass is the more surprising concept — a
  subscription for what pigeons *don't* do — while BreadCrumb, though
  vivid, is a straighter Uber Eats parody.
- **Q3: C10 beats C12.** The literal-seed-round pun is funnier and more
  pitch-like; the dating app's mate-for-life twist is good but less of an
  investor pitch.
- **Q4: C15 beats C14.** PigeonEye is more original; StepCoo's step joke is
  mild by comparison.

### Semi-finals (2 matches)

- **S1: C7 beats C2.** Both are poop jokes, but C7's is a full business
  inversion — paying pigeons to *withhold* poop — which is more original
  and surprising than C2's "poop on the competition," a known phrase.
- **S2: C10 beats C15.** The seed-round pun is funnier and more directly an
  investor pitch; PigeonEye reads more like a description of a capability.

### Final

- **F: C7 beats C10.** Both are on-subject, funny, and pigeon-voiced. C7
  wins on criteria 2 and 3: its joke is a complete, surprising business
  concept (a subscription selling the *absence* of pigeon poop) rather
  than a single pun, however good the seed-round pun is. C7 is also the
  tightest finalist at 126 characters with every word earning its place.
